LOOP-R: the five letters and the nine agents

An agent that evaluates its own work tends to approve itself. LOOP-R, the open INEMA framework that gives this track its name, solves this by separating roles: nine agents, each with what it reads, what it writes, what it decides and what it never does. The five letters give the order of the method. Here you see the design; promoting and rolling back versions belongs to course 5.

  1. 1/6

    L · Locate

    Pick a process that repeats a lot and write the goal with a number: take X to Y without worsening Z. The loop sheet has five answers that no system can safely infer: a goal with a number, where the evidence comes from, what never changes on its own, a cap on cost and time, and who approves. It is the goal from lesson 1, with a guardrail.

  2. 2/6

    O · Operate

    The process gets a current version, a short script followed in every run, so that two runs can be compared. The Executor produces the artifact and logs one line. It doesn't opine, doesn't suggest, doesn't change its own prompt.

  3. 3/6

    O · Observe

    One line per run, always the same columns, result as yes or no. The Observer turns the lines into evidence: counts by version and variant, no adjectives. If the data doesn't match the schema, it stops the cycle.

  4. 4/6

    P · Propose

    The Critic says what worked and what failed, with the strength of the evidence, without proposing a fix. The Optimizer writes hypotheses in the format SE, ENTÃO, PORQUE and MUDANÇA, without repeating discarded ones. The Guardian vetoes anything that touches what never changes or the cap; the veto is final and it doesn't negotiate.

  5. 5/6

    R · Reinforce

    The Experimenter designs the A versus B test, with the minimum sample calculated beforehand. The Evaluator answers one of three: B_GANHOU, A_SEGUE or AMOSTRA_INSUFICIENTE. There is no "it's winning". The Memory records everything without erasing, and the Meta-agent looks at the system (cost, vetoes) and only reports.

  6. 6/6

    Whoever builds is not whoever inspects

    Nine separate roles, each reading only what its contract says. It's the separation, not a cleverer prompt, that keeps the loop from convincing itself it improved.

In the vendas-whatsapp example, the agents really ran; the data is synthetic (1,500 generated rows) and the human decisions are simulated. The example's README separates what is real from what is simulated. Use it to read the mechanism, not as proof of gain.

03 · A discard that teaches

Cycle 0002 of the example: the metric went up and the new version still didn't make it in.

1. The hypothesis

Proposals of at most 80 words. It passed the Guardian and went to testing, A against B, 300 runs on each side.

2. The result

Response rate 19.3% in A against 29.7% in B: significant difference on the target.

But complaints: 6 in 300 against 1 in 300, with zero tolerance on the loop sheet.

3. The verdict

A_SEGUE: B only wins if the target goes up and no guardrail drops beyond the tolerance. Both together.

Without this rule, the loop learns to cheat the number.

4. The recorded lesson

The hypothesis went to discarded, with the numbers, so it won't be tested again. And the tolerance for rare events was corrected for the next tests, because zero doesn't separate effect from chance.

Promoting and rolling back a version is the subject of course 5.

07 · In three sentences

Five lettersLocate the process and the number, Operate the same way every time, Observe in lines, Propose with critique and veto, Reinforce only with a test.
Nine contractsEach agent reads, writes, and decides only what its contract says, and has a list of what it never does.
Non-regressionLOOP-R does not guarantee the number goes up; it guarantees that a worse version does not replace the current one by the system's decision.
